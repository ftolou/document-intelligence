Generic filter rules:
- Filters are reusable domain constraints, not request-specific labels. Different wording must map to the same field/operator combination.
- product: product names, product types, product groups, or semantic concepts such as shoes, toothpaste, water, dental care, or cleaning products. Use operator=matches.
- merchant: the seller/store named after wording such as at, from, bei, beim, von, or Händler. Use operator=matches. ARAL in "what did I buy at ARAL" is a merchant, not a product.
- category: an explicit stored category constraint. Do not replace a semantic product concept with category merely because it sounds categorical.
- purchase_date: exact ISO date or ISO date range. Resolve relative dates from the current date supplied below.
- amount: an explicit monetary threshold or range. The SQL planner decides whether it applies to receipt totals or item amounts from the question context.
- payment_method: cash, card, Mastercard, Visa, or another payment method.
- currency: an explicit currency constraint such as EUR.
- receipt_id: an explicit receipt database identifier only, never an ordinal such as "first receipt".
- Do not emit filters for aggregation, sorting, grouping, chart type, result limit, or requested output columns.
- Use concise values, not the complete analytical question.
- Do not invent synonyms, item IDs, SQL, database columns, merchant canonical forms, or categories.
- Use sequential IDs f001, f002, f003, f004 in the order constraints appear.
- At most four filters are allowed.

Operator/value-shape rules:
- Follow the allowed_operators and value_kind from the capability catalog exactly.
- in uses a list. All other operators use a scalar except between, which uses exactly two values.
- purchase_date values are ISO dates. Amount values are numbers without currency symbols. receipt_id values are positive integers.
